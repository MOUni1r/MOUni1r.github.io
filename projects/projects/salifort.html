Data foundation

The case study moves from duplicate handling and exploratory analysis to feature engineering, logistic regression baseline, Random Forest modeling, holdout evaluation, and business interpretation.

Data foundation

14,999 employee records and 10 variables were available. No missing values were found. 3,008 duplicate records were removed.

Feature engineering

An overworked indicator was created using a threshold above 175 monthly hours, matching the notebook framing.

Model comparison

Logistic Regression was used as a baseline. The feature-engineered Random Forest produced the stronger saved holdout result.

Decision support

The model is framed as a tool for human review and workforce investigation, not an automated employment decision system.

What the model says

The headline result is useful only when paired with the trade-offs and statistical context around it.

Key finding

Workload and evaluation variables carried strong predictive signal.

96.2% accuracy

The final feature-engineered Random Forest achieved 96.2% accuracy, 87.0% precision, 90.4% recall, 88.7% F1, and 93.8% ROC AUC on the saved holdout set. Important model features included last evaluation, number of projects, tenure, and the engineered overworked indicator.

05

What I'd recommend

The point of this analysis is what HR investigates next, not the accuracy number on its own.

01

Review workload and project allocation for employees carrying a high project count or long monthly hours. The engineered “overworked” flag is one of the strongest signals in the model.

02

Investigate whether long-tenure employees face different workload or career-development patterns than the rest of the workforce.

03

Look into why some employees post high evaluation scores while also logging very long hours. That combination shows up repeatedly among predicted turnover risk.

04

Route the model's output to HR for human review and follow-up conversations, not as an automated basis for employment decisions.

06

Limitations & next steps

A strong case study should make its boundaries visible.

01The dataset is educational, so the model should not be treated as a production workforce predictor.
02The model is predictive rather than causal and should not be interpreted as explaining why an employee leaves.
03The holdout was reused across iterative modeling rounds, so the reported metric is not an independent production estimate.
