01 · Analysis & evidence

Workload and evaluation carried strong predictive signal.

The workflow started with duplicate handling and exploratory analysis, then moved into feature engineering, a Logistic Regression baseline, Random Forest comparison, and holdout evaluation. The model is treated as decision support for human review, not an automated employment decision.

Key findingThe strongest signals were connected to workload and evaluation-related variables, giving HR a focused area to investigate instead of treating turnover as a black-box outcome.
02 · Model strategy

The method followed the question.

I used model comparison and evaluation to understand what the data could support, rather than treating the highest single score as the whole result.

03 · Client value

What this work can help a client decide.

The useful outcome is a way to prioritize the HR questions behind turnover, not a score that should make employment decisions automatically.

ProblemHigh turnover can be expensive, but raw employee records do not directly show where the risk is concentrated.
What the analysis addsWorkload, evaluation, and related signals can focus the next HR investigation on specific patterns.
Practical useUse the model and feature analysis to prioritize retention reviews, workload checks, and further data collection.
04 · Final conclusion

The result is useful when it changes the next decision.

The analysis suggests that turnover risk is strongly associated with a small set of workforce patterns. A practical next step is to investigate workload balance, evaluation, and employee experience with fresh HR data before making retention decisions.

What we learnedThe strongest signals were connected to workload and evaluation-related variables, giving HR a focused area to investigate instead of treating turnover as a black-box outcome.
What I would recommendThe analysis suggests that turnover risk is strongly associated with a small set of workforce patterns. A practical next step is to investigate workload balance, evaluation, and employee experience with fresh HR data before making retention decisions.
What to watchThe dataset is educational. The model shows predictive relationships, not causal proof. A real deployment would need fresh data, fairness checks, and validation with actual HR outcomes.
05 · Recommendation & limits

What I would do next.

Recommended next steps

  • Review workload, project allocation, and development patterns for employees showing stronger risk signals.
  • Validate the recommendation with fresh data and a clear business success metric.
