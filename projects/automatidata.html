01 · Analysis

Payment method was associated with average fare.

The workflow combined data-quality checks, trip-level EDA, statistical testing, leakage-aware feature engineering, cross-validated model selection, held-out evaluation, and error analysis. The payment comparison is observational, not a causal pricing rule.

Key findingThe card and cash groups showed a measurable average fare difference, while fare prediction remained difficult enough that errors should be treated as an operational signal rather than an exact estimate.

03 · Final conclusion

What a client could actually use from this work.

Client takeaway: The analysis gives a clearer view of fare behavior, payment-method differences, and prediction error, which can be used to decide where further investigation is most useful.

Key findingCard and cash groups showed a measurable average fare difference, while fare prediction remained difficult enough that errors matter operationally.
Business impactThe results can help identify fare patterns and cases that deserve review instead of relying only on overall averages or a single prediction.
Recommended actionUse the analysis to investigate fare behavior and flag high-error or high-fare cases for further review.
LimitationsThe payment gap is observational, not causal. The dataset is educational, and production use would require fresh validation and careful leakage controls.
